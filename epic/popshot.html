How it plays

Popshot combines fast first-person combat with simple construction. Each world has two enemy waves. Clear the first to start the second; defeat every rival in the final wave to unlock the next island. Bosses join the second wave in worlds 3, 6 and 10.

The campaign moves from Sunshine Shores and Candy Canyon to crystal, coral and volcanic environments, ending at Rainbow Citadel. Dinosaurs take more punishment and fire heavier shots, ninjas approach faster with weaving movement, and aliens fire paired energy shots. Later worlds mix those threats.

Your loadout

Pop Rifle and Boom Shotgun provide familiar ranged attacks. Splat Gun adds rapid colorful hits; Pop Grenades explode after a short fuse. Crossbow bolts and throwing stars are travelling projectiles. The sword has unlimited uses but short reach; the machine gun has a 60-shot magazine.

A better first run

  1. Move before you fire. Enemy projectiles travel through the world. Keep an escape route open and step sideways when an enemy winds up an attack.
  2. Match range to weapon. Use the crossbow for a deliberate heavy hit. Save sword swings and the shotgun for close encounters. The machine gun is useful when a fast ninja is difficult to track.
  3. Build with a purpose. A wall can interrupt incoming fire; a ramp gives you a route upward. You have 24 pieces per world, so avoid blocking your own path with unnecessary construction.
  4. Give grenades room. Their colorful blast can hurt you too. Throw from a safe distance rather than dropping one at your feet.
  5. Reset your approach to bosses. Spore King, the Ringmaster and Prism Titan use wider projectile fans. Watch for a gap instead of standing still and trading damage.

Devices, saves and known limits

Progress and best scores are stored in your browser. They do not transfer automatically to another device; clearing site data can remove them. Levels restart with fresh health and ammunition.

Desktop keyboard and mouse are recommended. Phones have a movement stick, swipe aiming and action buttons, but responsiveness depends on hardware. The game needs WebGL support. Fullscreen availability depends on the browser.

The game doesn’t appear. What should I try?

Open the standalone game link, refresh once, and use a current browser. If you still see a blank player, report the game name, browser, device and what happened through the catalogue’s report option.

Why won’t fullscreen work?

Fullscreen is controlled by your browser. Open the game in a new tab and use its fullscreen button. Some phone browsers restrict fullscreen; rotating to landscape can still give the game more space.
