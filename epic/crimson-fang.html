How it plays

Crimson Fang is built around positioning and short bursts of aggression. Move through the stage, clear enemies along the road and advance toward the final arena. Entering that arena starts the boss encounter; defeating the boss lets you continue to the next stage.

The sword rewards repeated attacks with a three-hit combination. A ninja leap changes your position above the battlefield, while the dash gives a brief protected movement window. Shuriken let you attack at range without committing to a sword exchange. Spirit powers offer stronger options as you earn kill unlocks.

Boss encounters and music

Synth Samurai accompanies active stages at a low volume. Final Boss replaces it when a living boss is reached. The tracks do not overlap and follow the Sound toggle and pause state. Boss attacks vary between area attacks, charging movement and projectile patterns, so use the warning rather than assuming every arena plays the same way.

A better first run

  1. Watch the ground before committing. Boss attacks show a warning before they resolve. Move away from the marked location, then return to sword range.
  2. Use the third sword hit deliberately. Repeated attacks build a combination. Stay close enough to connect, but leave space to dash away when the opponent begins its attack.
  3. Change lanes. You can move vertically as well as advance right. Moving above or below an approaching enemy can be safer than running directly into it.
  4. Keep shuriken for awkward approaches. Ranged attacks let you pressure enemies while you reposition. They are useful when closing distance would place you inside a warning area.
  5. Budget spirit. Special moves share a cooldown and cost spirit. A powerful move at the wrong moment can leave you without a response during the next exchange.

Devices, saves and known limits

Stage Select lets you choose any of the ten stages. Best scores and kill unlocks use local browser storage where available. Private browsing or clearing site data may reset them.

Touch buttons are included, but keyboard controls provide the full set of combinations. A separate game tab gives more room than the catalogue player. Fullscreen is browser-dependent. Music starts after a user interaction where the browser requires it.

The game doesn’t appear. What should I try?

Open the standalone game link, refresh once, and use a current browser. If you still see a blank player, report the game name, browser, device and what happened through the catalogue’s report option.

Why won’t fullscreen work?

Fullscreen is controlled by your browser. Open the game in a new tab and use its fullscreen button. Some phone browsers restrict fullscreen; rotating to landscape can still give the game more space.
