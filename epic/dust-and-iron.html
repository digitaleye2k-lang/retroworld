How it plays

Dust & Iron replaces reflex-driven combat with deliberate orders. Select a crew member, choose a destination and watch the route through cover. You can stop the action in plan mode to think before the next move.

The current campaign offers ten contracts and three difficulty settings: Trail, Dust and Iron. Trail reduces the pressure from guards; Iron increases their health, movement and detection strength. Read each contract’s briefing and objective panel before moving—your route should serve that mission, not simply clear every guard.

The original Mesa Junction scenario introduced the western heist: cut the relay, take the ledger and regroup for extraction. The current campaign expands beyond that first demo. Its contract briefings are the source of truth for each mission.

The crew

June Mercer: scout with a pistol and Sling Shot, a ranged stun. Silas Rook: rifleman with a Lasso for a close guard. Tomás Garza: breacher with a shotgun and Smoke Bomb to block sight. Weapon ranges and movement speeds differ, so a single formation does not suit every passage.

A better first run

  1. Start on Trail to learn the orders. Use the first contract to practise selection, movement and plan mode before increasing guard pressure.
  2. Pause and inspect a route. Plan mode freezes patrols. Look for cover, narrow passages and a return path before sending the whole team forward.
  3. Assign jobs by range. June’s sling shot can stun a guard from range. Silas’s lasso works closer in. Tomás’s smoke can block sight when the group needs to cross an exposed lane.
  4. Avoid unnecessary shooting. Your starting ammunition is limited. Use the mission objective to decide whether a guard needs to be fought, stunned or avoided.
  5. Check the entire team. A safe route for one hand does not guarantee that the slower members have reached cover. Regroup before advancing into another sight line.

Devices, saves and known limits

This is an experimental tactical campaign. Mouse and keyboard are recommended because selection, context orders and camera movement are central to play. A touchscreen or TV remote should not be assumed to reproduce all desktop controls.

The mission reset starts that contract over. Read the active objective and on-screen field notes after an update: these can change as the prototype develops. Fullscreen needs browser support; opening the game in its own tab is an alternative when an embedded player is cramped.

The game doesn’t appear. What should I try?

Open the standalone game link, refresh once, and use a current browser. If you still see a blank player, report the game name, browser, device and what happened through the catalogue’s report option.

Why won’t fullscreen work?

Fullscreen is controlled by your browser. Open the game in a new tab and use its fullscreen button. Some phone browsers restrict fullscreen; rotating to landscape can still give the game more space.
